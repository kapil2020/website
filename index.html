01 About

A researcher who ships

Seven years spent moving between survey design, econometrics, machine learning and production code — because a model that never leaves the notebook never changes a commute.

I completed my Ph.D. in Transportation Engineering at IIT Kharagpur under Prof. Arkopal K. Goswami, where my dissertation asked a deceptively simple question: when the air is bad, how do people change the way they travel — and who is able to change at all? Answering it took a national-scale stated-preference survey, hybrid latent-class choice models, and a good deal of code.

I am now a Postdoctoral Researcher in the HUMAN Lab at the University of California, Los Angeles, working with Prof. Youngseo Kim on human-centred models of urban mobility. The through-line of my work has not changed: take behaviour seriously, measure exposure honestly, and build the tool that makes the finding usable.

That last part matters to me. The DRUM routing engine — a web application that returns the cleanest, not merely the fastest, route across an Indian city — began as a chapter of my thesis, was covered by The Hindu, and was presented at Google India in 2025. Earlier work with Prof. Amit Agarwal at IIT Roorkee and as a consultant at WRI India shaped how I think about the policy end of the pipeline.

Outside the lab I write, run, and cycle — the last of which is, conveniently, also a research interest.

02 Research

Six threads, one question

How do people choose to move, what does that choice cost them in exposure and health, and how do we redesign the system so the good choice is also the easy one?

All work on Scholar ›
01 / Exposure

Air-quality exposure

Quantifying what commuters actually inhale door-to-door, and how perceived and measured air quality reshape the trips they are willing to make.

Core research Studies ›
02 / Routing

Sustainable routing

DRUM: a live routing engine that offers the least-polluted and lowest-emission path instead of only the fastest one, built and deployed for Indian cities.

Lead developer Live app ›
03 / Behaviour

Travel-behaviour modelling

Discrete choice and hybrid latent-class models that separate travellers who can change their behaviour from those who merely say they would.

Principal investigator Survey ›
04 / Learning

Machine learning for mobility

Bridging econometric theory and deep learning — interpretable predictors of mode choice, next-day PM2.5, and pollutant fields across a city.

ML engineer Paper ›
05 / Active mobility

Walking & cycling

Health-impact assessment and choice modelling for first/last-mile access, walkability scoring from street imagery, and the case for building for bikes.

Researcher Paper ›
06 / Data

Urban data science

Large spatial-temporal pipelines, dashboards and open tooling that make mobility and air-quality data legible to planners rather than only to modellers.
