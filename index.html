Methods

PD‑MUSE

Primal–Dual Maximum-Utility Structured Estimator

Nested and inverse product-differentiation logit models buy realistic substitution patterns at the cost of a likelihood that is no longer concave. PD‑MUSE recasts estimation as a convex program, and confines what remains to a bounded, low-dimensional search.

HEAT

Heat · Exposure · Activity · Travel

As cities warm, people shift trips earlier, shorten them, or change mode — and those who cannot absorb the heat instead. HEAT connects thermal stress to how days are scheduled and how journeys are actually made.

Research programme

A single question, four directions

My work spans transportation engineering, environmental health, behavioural science and applied machine learning. Below: the programme, then what it has found.

One question, approached from four directions: how do people decide to move, and what does that decision cost them?

Answering it takes the instruments of transportation engineering to measure, behavioural and decision science to explain, statistics and machine learning to generalise, and software engineering to deliver. I work across all four — and I build the instruments as well as the models, because a finding that never reaches a commuter or a planner has not finished its job.

The seasonal experiment

Winter decides who reroutes

The core of my dissertation is a two-wave route-choice panel: the same 723 Kolkata commuters surveyed once in winter and again in summer, yielding 5,224 choice observations. Following the same people across seasons separates a real change in preference from a change in who happens to answer.

Air quality weighs roughly four times more heavily on route choice in winter than in summer, and willingness to pay for cleaner air is 1.8× higher per AQI band. Commuters valued a pre-trip AQI alert more than an equivalent reduction in physical exposure — which makes real-time air-quality information the cheapest policy lever available. AQI literacy, not income, is the dominant moderator.
